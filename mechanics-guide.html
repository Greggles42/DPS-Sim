DPS Sim Mechanics Guide

This page explains exactly how this simulator models melee damage, hit chance, haste, offense, and target defenses. Formulas are based on EQMacEmu combat logic and this simulator's implementation.

1) Weapon Base Damage and Delay

Every weapon starts from base damage and delay. Delay is in deciseconds (40 = 4.0s).

effectiveDelayDecisec = max(4, delayDecisec / (1 + effectiveHastePercent / 100))

Faster effective delay means more swings per minute and generally more DPS.

2) Threat and TPS (not the same as DPS)

The header shows Total TPS (Beta) — approximate threat per second. It is not your damage per second; it is a separate aggregate used to reason about tank snap aggro and weapon proc contribution.

2.1) What “total threat” is

Over a simulated fight of length T seconds, the sim accumulates integer threat from allowed sources. The report value is:

totalThreat = swingThreatAcc + procThreatAcc

Ranged uses the same idea: bow shot swing threat plus ranged proc threat.

Displayed TPS uses:

TPS = totalThreat / T

So TPS is an average rate over the whole fight. It scales with how often you generate swing hate and proc hate, not with the size of individual damage rolls (for swings).

2.2) Components of total threat (melee)

  1. Swing threat (primary hand) — On every main-hand swing attempt (hit or miss), add max(0, cappedWeaponDamage + mainHandDamageBonus). This matches EQMacEmu-style pre-roll hate from displayed base + damage bonus (threat.js: meleeSwingThreatPrimary).
  2. Swing threat (off hand) — On each off-hand swing attempt (hit or miss), add max(0, cappedOffhandWeaponDamage) only — no main-hand damage bonus on the secondary slot (meleeSwingThreatOffhand).
  3. Special attacks (backstab, flying kick, kick, bash, etc.) — Yes, they count. Each time a special fires on its cooldown, the sim adds one primary-hand swing hate event — the same amount as a normal main-hand auto (weapon base + main-hand damage bonus), including on a miss. Special damage does not add extra threat in this model: a huge backstab number does not increase swing hate beyond that single MH-sized slice.
  4. Monk fistweaving — Weaved punches add swing threat as their configured fist base (epic vs non-epic), using the same “primary style” helper as other MH swing hate for those events (meleeSwingThreatPrimary(fistBase, 0)).
  5. Proc threat (direct damage spells) — When a weapon proc deals instant DD, hate from damage uses the spell’s base proc damage (before resists / Spell Casting Fury crit), then applies the per-event cap (default 400). DoT procs accrue threat from unresisted per-tick base (total proc damage ÷ tick count), not from resisted tick damage.
  6. Proc threat (DD + stun) — If spell data includes both HP damage and a stun (EQEmu SE 21), the sim adds an additional mob–max-HP detrimental slice (min(1200, max(25, floor(targetMaxHp / 15)))) on top of the DD-based proc hate (e.g. Thunder Strike).
  7. Proc threat (detrimental, no HP damage) — For procs classified as detrimental with no SE_CurrentHP damage in spell data: min(1200, max(25, floor(targetMaxHp / 15))). targetMaxHp comes from the selected NPC’s hp when you pick a target from search; otherwise a generic HP curve (e.g. 22,105 at mob level 60, scaled by level).
  8. Flat proc hate (SE 92) — effect_base_value on SE 92 is applied once per instant proc (not each DoT tick), signed: positive adds hate, negative reduces it (e.g. Khalshazar’s Deceit). Spells that are only SE 92 (no HP damage) do not use the max-HP/15 non-DD bucket—they use this flat value only.

2.3) Components of total threat (ranged)

  • Per shot: swing threat ≈ bow displayed damage + arrow displayed damage (same idea as “base” before the hit roll).
  • Procs: same DD base / cap / non-DD detrimental / flat hate rules as melee, on the bow object.

2.4) What is not in this TPS

  • Threat from casting player spells, heals, buffs, or non-weapon abilities (not simulated).
  • Separate “damage-based” hate for specials (the sim does not add threat equal to special hit damage).
  • Rune absorb, mez/root flat hate tables, etc., unless modeled elsewhere in the future.

2.5) Practical example (melee, rounded)

Suppose a 60-second fight, main hand only, no offhand, no procs, no specials: weapon shows 15 damage, main-hand damage bonus 35, effective delay 2.0s → about 30 main-hand swings. Each swing adds swing hate 15 + 35 = 50.

  • swingThreatAcc ≈ 30 × 50 = 1,500
  • procThreatAcc = 0
  • totalThreat = 1,500 → TPS ≈ 1,500 / 60 = 25.0

If you also enable a special on cooldown (e.g. kick) and it fires 6 times in 60s, each attempt adds another 50 swing hate (same as one MH auto), even if some miss:

  • Extra from specials: 6 × 50 = 300
  • totalThreat ≈ 1,500 + 300 = 1,800 → TPS ≈ 30.0

If the weapon then procs an instant DD with base 200 and default cap 400, each proc adds up to 200 to procThreatAcc (not reduced by resists in the threat math). Ten procs would add 2,000 proc threat on top of swing threat, pushing TPS up accordingly.

Implementation: threat.js + combat.js. Reference: EQMacEmu-style swing and proc hate (approximate; not identical to live in every edge case).

3) Haste

Actual haste rules used by the simulator:

  1. UI worn-haste cap by low level:
    wornHasteUsed = (level <= 25) ? min(wornHasteInput, 10) : wornHasteInput
  2. Raw haste passed into combat:
    rawHaste = wornHasteUsed + spellHasteInput
  3. Positive haste cap by level (combat logic):
    capTotal = 100 + (level > 59 ? 100 : (level > 50 ? 85 : level + 25))
    v3Cap = (level > 50 ? 25 : 10)
    maxBonus = (capTotal - 100) + v3Cap
    effectiveHastePercent = (rawHaste < 0) ? rawHaste : min(rawHaste, maxBonus)
  4. Delay conversion with minimum delay floor:
    effectiveDelayDecisec = max(4, delayDecisec / (1 + effectiveHastePercent / 100))

    Actual result examples:

    delay=40, effectiveHaste=0% -> effectiveDelay=40 decisec (4.00s)
    delay=40, effectiveHaste=41% -> effectiveDelay=28.37 decisec (2.84s)
    delay=10, effectiveHaste=200% -> computed 3.33 decisec, clamped to 4 decisec (0.40s floor)

Haste changes attack frequency, not hit chance directly.

4) Offense Skill vs Offense Rating (Important)

These are different and used in different parts of the calculation:

  • Offense skill (0-255): used in to-hit math.
  • Offense rating: used in damage roll math.
toHit = 7 + offenseSkill + weaponSkill + toHitBonus
offenseRating = offenseSkill + STR_bonus + wornATK + spellATK

STR contributes through:

STR_bonus = floor((2 * STR - 150) / 3), when STR >= 75

Stat cap: STR and DEX are capped at 255 before contributing to offense rating. The Planar Power AA (PoP, ranks 1–5) raises this cap by +5 per rank (up to 280 at rank 5). The cap is enforced at simulation time for both melee (STR → offense rating) and ranged (DEX → archery offense rating).

5) Hit Chance (Avoidance) vs Damage (Mitigation)

AC is not a single value in this sim. It is split into two defense concepts:

  • Avoidance: controls whether you hit/miss.
  • Mitigation: controls how hard a landed hit is reduced.

Avoidance (hit chance)

effectiveToHit = min(toHit, 550)
a = effectiveToHit + 10; b = avoidance + 10
if a*1.21 > b: hitChance = 1 - b / (a*1.21*2); else hitChance = (a*1.21) / (b*2)

Tactical Mastery AA (Warrior, PoP): reduces the NPC's effective avoidance by 10/20/30 at rank 1/2/3 before the hit-chance formula runs. A lower avoidance value shifts the hit-chance formula in the attacker's favour, increasing landed-hit rate.

Mitigation (damage size)

Mitigation is the defender value used only for the damage roll (how hard a landed hit hits). It does not affect hit chance. It is computed from mob level and target AC, then used in the RollD20 contest below.

How mitigation is computed (GetMitigation):

  • Level < 15: mit = level × 3 (and +2 if level < 3).
  • Level ≥ 15: mit = floor(level × 41 / 10) − 15.
  • Capped at 200. If the result is 200 and mob AC > 200, use mob AC instead.
  • Then add item/spell AC bonuses: + floor(4×itemAC/3) + floor(spellAC/4).

rollIndex in detail (RollD20)

For each landed hit, the simulator rolls a rollIndex from 1 to 20. That index decides where the hit lands in the damage range:

rollIndex = RollD20(offenseRating, mitigation) // result 1..20
damage = floor((rollIndex × baseDamage + 5) / 10) + damageBonus

So rollIndex = 1 gives minimum damage; rollIndex = 20 gives maximum damage (for that weapon’s base range, before crits etc.).

How rollIndex is derived (the atk/def roll competition):

  1. Attack roll: atkRoll = random(0, offenseRating+5) — uniform integer from 0 up to (offenseRating+5)−1.
  2. Defense roll: defRoll = random(0, mitigation+5) — uniform integer from 0 up to (mitigation+5)−1.
  3. Average scale: avg = floor((offenseRating + mitigation + 10) / 2).
  4. Raw contest: raw = (atkRoll − defRoll) + floor(avg/2), then scaled and clamped into 0..19, and rollIndex = raw + 1 so the final value is 1..20.

What causes a hit to hit for max vs min?

  • High damage (max or near-max): When atkRoll is high and defRoll is low, (atkRoll − defRoll) is large, so the scaled index is high (near 20). So you get max (or near-max) hits when the attacker “wins” the roll contest by a wide margin.
  • Low damage (min or near-min): When atkRoll is low and defRoll is high, (atkRoll − defRoll) is small or negative, so the scaled index is low (near 1). So you get min (or near-min) hits when the defender “wins” the roll contest.

Higher offense rating (STR, worn ATK, spell ATK, offense skill) increases the range of atkRoll, so you can roll higher and get more big hits. Higher mitigation (e.g. high-AC or high-level mobs) increases the range of defRoll, so the defender often “wins” more and you see more low (min) hits and fewer max hits. That is why high-mitigation targets feel like they reduce your damage spread and push hits toward the bottom of the range.

6) In Front vs Behind

Choosing Behind does not force all swings to land. Misses still happen from to-hit vs avoidance.

  • Behind: skips block/parry/riposte/dodge layer.
  • Front: after a successful hit roll, there is an additional avoid chance.
front extra avoid layer = 8%

Use front if you want a conservative/tank-facing estimate; use behind for DPS-from-rear assumptions.

7) Core Max-Hit Formulas

For a single landed non-crit swing before proc damage:

minNonCrit ~= floor((1 * baseDamage + 5) / 10) + damageBonus
maxNonCrit ~= floor((20 * baseDamage + 5) / 10) + damageBonus

So the non-crit range is roughly ~0.1x to ~2.0x of baseDamage (plus damage bonus).

Then multipliers/crit logic can further scale landed damage.

7.5) Double Attack, Triple Attack, and Dual Wield

Double attack (main-hand extra swing)

The simulator computes:

doubleAttackEffective = getDoubleAttackEffective(level, doubleAttackSkill) + ferocityDaBonus
doubleAttack succeeds if doubleAttackEffective > randomInt(0, 499)

Approximate chance:

P(doubleAttack) ~= doubleAttackEffective / 500

Ferocity AA (War/Rng/Monk/Rog, PoP): adds +10 to doubleAttackEffective per rank (+2% DA chance per rank, up to rank 3 = +6%).

Knight's Advantage AA (Pal/SHD, PoP): adds +10/+20/+30 to doubleAttackEffective per rank (+2%/+4%/+6% DA chance). Stacks with Ferocity.

Speed of the Knight AA (Pal/SHD, PoP): after a double attack, there is a 2%/4%/8% chance at rank 1/2/3 to perform an extra primary-hand 2H attack. Requires a 2H weapon equipped.

Bard and Beastlord are excluded from the normal double-attack skill check. Instead they use a flat AA-driven chance:

  • Harmonious Attack (Bard, PoP): +2% flat DA chance per rank (up to rank 5 = +10%).
  • Bestial Frenzy (Beastlord, PoP): +2% flat DA chance per rank (up to rank 5 = +10%).

These flat chances are rolled independently after the normal DA check fails for those classes.

Triple attack (warrior/monk only)

  • Only Warrior and Monk at level 60+.
  • Only checked on rounds where double attack already succeeded.
P(triple | double) = 13.5%
P(triple on round) ~= P(doubleAttack) * 0.135

Raging Flurry AA (Warrior, PoP): on rounds where triple attack succeeds, there is a 10%/20%/30% chance (rank 1/2/3) of an additional primary-hand flurry swing. Requires level 60+.

Dual wield (offhand round chance)

Offhand rounds are gated by dual-wield chance:

dualWieldEffective = dualWieldSkill + level + ambidexterityBonus
dualWield succeeds if dualWieldEffective > randomInt(0, 374)
P(offhand round) ~= dualWieldEffective / 375

Ambidexterity AA (Luclin): adds +32 to dualWieldEffective (approximately +8.5% offhand round chance at max DW skill).

Independent offhand timer

Offhand swing timing is independent from main hand.

  • Main hand advances on its own timer from weapon 1 delay.
  • Offhand advances on its own timer from weapon 2 delay.
  • The offhand initial swing is randomly offset, then repeats independently.

This means offhand does not "wait" for main-hand events.

7.6) Technique of Master Wu (Monk, PoP)

After each Flying Kick attempt (hit or miss), if Technique of Master Wu is trained, two independent rolls determine whether bonus strikes fire. Source: EQMacEmu special_attacks.cpp.

wuChance = masterWuRank × 10 (integer percent: 10/20/30/40/50 at ranks 1–5)

The two rolls are independent (not a chain):

  1. Main roll — rng() × 100 < wuChance. If it passes, fire 1 extra monk strike.
  2. Secondary roll — rng() × 100 < wuChance / 4 (independent of the main roll). If it passes, fire 1 additional extra strike.

Each extra strike is randomly selected from four monk combat skills:

SkillSkill base damage (max skill 225)Min damage formula
Flying Kick29max(1, level × 4 / 5)
Eagle Strike111
Tiger Claw81
Round Kick91

Eagle Strike, Tiger Claw, and Round Kick are assumed at max skill (225). Each extra strike rolls its own hit/miss check, damage roll, and crit roll independently.

The report's Technique of Master Wu section shows: Flying Kicks with Wu active, main-roll trigger count, extra strikes attempted/landed per skill, total Wu damage, Wu DPS, and average Wu damage per FK use compared to the average base FK hit.

7.7) Flurry AA (Warrior, Luclin)

Flurry gives a chance at up to 2 additional primary-hand attacks per round, in addition to normal double/triple attack. Ranks 1/2/3 scale the flurry chance.

Flurry attacks use the same primary-hand damage roll as normal swings. They are independent of the double/triple attack flow.

7.8) Slay Undead (Paladin AA, Luclin)

Slay Undead is a Paladin-exclusive class AA (3 ranks, available from Luclin onward). On each melee swing that lands against an undead target, the engine first rolls for Slay Undead before checking for a regular melee crit. If the slay roll succeeds the hit becomes a Slay hit and the normal crit path is skipped entirely for that swing. It is not a proc — it rides the melee swing itself, requires no weapon and fires no spell.

Eligible targets

The target must have one of the undead body types: 3 (Undead), 8 (Undead Pet), or 12 (Vampire). If the target does not match, the Slay roll is never attempted.

Roll chance per swing

One flat-percent roll is made independently for every individual swing that hits — each primary-hand hit, double-attack hit, triple-attack hit, and flurry hit all get their own roll. Offhand swings are not checked (Slay only applies to weapon1 / primary hand).

RankSlay chanceDamage multiplier
Rank 12.25%15× (1500%)
Rank 22.35%16× (1600%)
Rank 32.40%17× (1700%)

Damage formula

When the slay roll succeeds, the hit damage is replaced by:

slayDmg = floor( (rolledDamage − damageBonus + 6) × multiplierPct / 100 ) + damageBonus

A minimum floor is also enforced to prevent extremely weak rolls from deflating the slay:

minSlay = floor( (minBase + 5) × multiplierPct / 100 ) + damageBonus
finalDmg = max(slayDmg, minSlay)

Where rolledDamage is the weapon roll after all modifiers (including STR/ATK scaling) but before the crit multiplier, damageBonus is the melee damage bonus from STR/ATK/offense, and minBase = 1 (the minimum weapon base roll).

The +6 offset in the numerator reflects the EQMac source convention for undead slay damage and keeps slay hits proportional even on minimum rolls.

Interaction with crits and other multipliers

If the slay roll fires, the swing is recorded as a Slay hit and the regular melee crit roll is not attempted. SCF (Spell Casting Fury) and Combat Fury crit rolls do not apply to a slay hit. A slay hit also suppresses any additional per-swing damage bonuses that would normally be gated on the crit result. In practice the slay multiplier (15–17×) is large enough that missing the crit is negligible.

What the report shows

The sim report's Slay Undead (AA) section lists:

  • Slay Undead hits — total number of swings where the slay roll fired.
  • Slay Undead damage — cumulative damage from all slay hits.
  • Max Slay Undead hit — single largest slay hit seen across all runs.
  • DPS from Slay Undead — slay damage ÷ fight duration.

Slay Undead only appears in the report when the selected class is Paladin. The section is omitted for all other classes.

7.9) Two Hand Bash (Paladin / Shadow Knight, Luclin)

By default, Paladin and Shadow Knight characters cannot bash while wielding a two-handed weapon in the primary slot — the bash special attack is suppressed. The Two Hand Bash Luclin class AA removes this restriction, enabling bash on cooldown regardless of weapon type.

If the AA is not trained and a 2H weapon is equipped, the sim sets canFireSpecial = false for the bash attack type, so no bash attempts fire during the simulation.

7.10) 2H Weave (Warrior / Ranger / Beastlord)

Warriors, Rangers, and Beastlords can optionally "weave" an offhand 1H weapon alongside a two-handed primary. Enable this by checking 2H Weave in the weapon section and equipping a 1H weapon in the offhand slot.

After every main-hand 2H swing, the sim makes one offhand swing attempt using the equipped 1H weapon. The offhand attempt goes through a normal dual-wield round check (based on your dual-wield skill), then a full hit/miss resolution using the 1H weapon's stats. No main-hand damage bonus applies to the weaved offhand swing.

This is identical in structure to the Monk non-epic fistweave — it uses the same weave-mode code path in the engine, just with a 1H weapon in place of fists.

The offhand weapon must be a 1H weapon type (not a 2H or bow). The dual-wield skill field must be non-zero. If either condition is unmet, 2H Weave has no effect.

8) Elemental Damage on Weapons

Elemental weapon damage is treated as an extra base-damage adder before the melee damage roll. It is first reduced by target resist for that element (MR/FR/CR/PR/DR).

if resist > 200: elementalDamage = 0
roll = randomInt(1, 201) - resist
if roll < 1: elementalDamage = 0; if 1..99: elementalDamage = floor(weaponElem * roll/100); else elementalDamage = weaponElem

Then this adder is included in swing base damage:

baseDamageForSwing = weaponBaseDamage + elementalDamage(+ bane if enabled)

High target resists can heavily suppress elemental contribution; this is why elemental-heavy weapons can vary by target profile.

Elemental resist rolls use a dedicated RNG stream (separate from the main combat RNG used for hit/damage rolls). This ensures that equipping a weapon with elemental damage in one slot has no effect on the RNG sequence seen by the other slot's hit and damage rolls.

9) Proc Chance and Proc Resists

Proc chance per swing/round

Proc chance scales with effective delay and DEX. Offhand has a dual-wield adjustment. Then proc-rate modifiers are applied.

baseProcChance = (0.0004166667 + 1.1437908496732e-5 * DEX) * effectiveDelay
offhandAdjusted = baseProcChance * (50 / dualWieldChance)
finalProcChance = clamp(0, 1, adjustedChance * (100 + procRate) / 100)

Level-gated procs do not fire below required proc level.

Proc spell resist resolution

For resistable proc spells, the sim checks immunity and then runs a resist calculation using target resist type (MR/FR/CR/DR/PR), level delta, and spell resist modifier.

resistChance = clamp(0, 200, targetResist + levelMod + resistModifier)
if roll > resistChance: full land (100% damage)
if roll <= resistChance: full resist (0%) or partial based on no-partial flag

Resulting proc damage is:

actualProcDamage = floor(baseProcDamage * effectiveness / 100)

So procs can be:

  • Full land: full listed proc damage.
  • Partial resist: reduced proc damage.
  • Full resist: 0 proc damage.

This is why two weapons with similar listed proc damage can perform differently against different target resist profiles.

Soul Abrasion AA (Shadow Knight, Luclin, ranks 1–3): multiplies the base proc spell damage by 1.10 / 1.20 / 1.30 before the resist roll and damage calculation. Applied to both primary and offhand proc weapons.

10) Ranged (Bow + Arrow) — Damage Model

The ranged tab simulates bow + arrow archery for a level 60 Ranger. The damage pipeline follows special_attacks.cpp Mob::DoArcheryAttackDmg and attack.cpp CalcMeleeDamage.

10.0) Archery damage pipeline

In order, for each shot that hits:

  1. Physical base: bow.damage + arrow.damage. This is multiplied by RuleR(Combat, ArcheryBaseDamageBonus) (default 1.0 on Project Quarm — no change).
  2. Archery Mastery AA (aabonuses.ArcheryDamageModifier):
    physicalBase += floor(physicalBase × masteryPct / 100)
    Rank 0 = +0%, Rank 1 = +30%, Rank 2 = +60%, Rank 3 = +100%.
  3. Trueshot discipline (spellbonuses.ArcheryDamageModifier = 105):
    physicalBase += floor(physicalBase × 105 / 100)
    Effectively doubles the base (2.05×). Only active during the Trueshot window.
  4. Archery halving — CalcMeleeDamage (attack.cpp) explicitly halves the base for SkillArchery before the D20 roll:
    if (baseDamage > 1) baseDamage /= 2
    This is the server's balancing rule for archery. At Archery Mastery rank 3 (+100%), the halving is exactly offset, so a maxed ranger shoots for the same base as the raw bow+arrow value.
  5. D20 damage roll — same RollD20(offenseRating, mitigation) contest as melee (see Section 5).
    damage = floor((roll × shotBase + 5) / 10)
  6. Ranger 51+ stationary bonus (see below) — applied after the D20 roll.
  7. RollDamageMultiplier — same level/class offense-based extra-hit chance as melee (see Section 7). The Warrior damage++ at level 55+ is explicitly excluded for archery.
  8. Critical hit — Ranger archery crit: 1.35 + DEX/34% (at DEX 255 ≈ 8.8%). Requires level > 16.

10.0.1) Max shot damage formula

The D20 roll produces a value from 1–20; roll = 20 gives the maximum non-crit hit. There is no melee damage bonus for archery (damageBonus = 0), so:

shotBase = floor((bow + arrow) × (1 + masteryPct/100) / 2)
maxNonCritShot = floor((20 × shotBase + 5) / 10)

At Archery Mastery rank 3 (+100%), the mastery exactly offsets the server ÷2, so shotBase = bow + arrow and max non-crit = floor((20 × (bow+arrow) + 5) / 10) ≈ 2 × (bow+arrow).

With the Ranger stationary bonus the post-D20 result is doubled before the crit roll, so:

maxNonCritShot (stationary) = 2 × floor((20 × shotBase + 5) / 10)

10.1) Ranger 51+ Stationary Bonus (beacon.cpp)

When a Ranger above level 50 shoots a target that is an NPC, not currently moving, and not temporarily rooted, the arrow deals double damage. This is implemented in beacon.cpp Beacon::Update and fires before TryCriticalHit, so the doubled value feeds into the multiplier and crit rolls.

if (Ranger && level > 50 && target.isNPC && !target.IsMoving() && (!target.IsRooted() || target.PermaRooted()))
weapon_dmg *= 2

Server rules on Project Quarm:

  • ArcheryBonusRequiresStationary = true — stationary check is enforced.
  • UseArcheryBonusRoll = false — no random roll; the bonus fires unconditionally when the stationary check passes.
  • ArcheryBonusChance = 50 — only relevant when UseArcheryBonusRoll = true (not active here).

The Mob stationary checkbox in the ranged tab controls whether this bonus applies in the simulation. It is checked by default because most raid and camp targets are stationary. Uncheck it when simulating a kiting or moving target.

10.2) Archery offense rating (GetOffense for SkillArchery)

Archery uses DEX, not STR, for the stat bonus:

offenseRating = archerySkill + floor((2×DEX − 150) / 3) + wornATK + spellATK (when DEX ≥ 75)

Rangers above level 54 also receive a linear bonus:

rangerBonus = level × 4 − 216 (e.g. +24 at level 60, +44 at level 65)

10.3) Quiver haste

Applied separately from standard haste (not subject to the 125% cap). If a quiver provides weight reduction (WR%), quiver haste = WR% ÷ 4 ÷ 100.

bowDelayReduction = floor(quiverHaste × speedMs) + 1
if (speedMs − reduction > 1000ms): effectiveDelay = speedMs − reduction

The Sony rule prevents the quiver from pushing delay below 1000ms (1 second).

10.4) Separating base vs proc damage (for ranking)

The ranged tab also answers: how much DPS should be credited to the bow vs the arrow so they can be ranked independently?

rangedTotal = report.ranged.totalDamage
procTotal = report.ranged.procDamageTotal
baseTotal = max(0, rangedTotal − procTotal)
  • baseTotal is all hit damage from bow base damage + arrow damage + any bane or elemental terms.
  • procTotal is all damage from bow procs (spell procs tied to the bow).
baseDps = baseTotal / durationSec
procDps = procTotal / durationSec

10.5) Splitting base DPS between bow and arrow

The simulator assumes that, before procs, the bow and arrow contribute proportionally to base DPS according to their relative base damages:

bowBase = bowDamage (+ bane if "Apply Bane" is checked)
arrowBase = arrow.damage
sumBase = max(1, bowBase + arrowBase)
bowShare = bowBase / sumBase
arrowShare = arrowBase / sumBase

Base DPS is then split using these shares, while all proc DPS is credited to the bow:

arrowDps = baseDps × arrowShare
bowDps = baseDps × bowShare + procDps
totalDps = bowDps + arrowDps

This preserves the total ranged DPS from the full simulation while giving a per-combo breakdown of how much the bow and arrow each contribute. The ranged ranking UI shows these values as Bow DPS, Arrow DPS, and Total DPS.

10.6) Ranking bows and arrows separately (slot mode)

To rank bows and arrows independently, the simulator first computes DPS for a grid of bow+arrow combinations, then derives slot values from those combos:

  • Bow slot DPS: for each bow ID, find the bow+arrow combo with the highest totalDps using that bow.
  • Arrow slot DPS: for each arrow ID, find the bow+arrow combo with the highest totalDps using that arrow.

In slot mode, the ranged ranking table shows:

  • Rows labeled Bow: <name> with the best total DPS that bow can achieve in any tested combo (and its bow/arrow DPS split).
  • Rows labeled Arrow: <name> with the best total DPS that arrow can achieve in any tested combo (and its bow/arrow DPS split).

This mirrors the melee slot-ranking behavior but is tailored for bows/arrows.

10.7) Reducing permutations: top-K bow selection

The full grid of combinations is #bows × #arrows. To keep the simulation responsive while still ranking arrows meaningfully, the simulator prunes bows using a simple damage/delay heuristic:

baseDmg = bow.damage + (bow.baneDamage or 0)
score = baseDmg / max(1, bow.delay)

Bows are sorted descending by score, and only the top K (currently 40) are used when building the bow+arrow combination grid for ranged ranking. Arrows are still tested against these high-quality bows, which is sufficient to determine a good relative ordering for arrows while dramatically reducing total simulations.

11) What ATK and AC Usually Do (Prioritization)

Stat Main Effect When It Matters Most
Offense skill / weapon skill Raises hit chance via to-hit. Most noticeable when accuracy is not near cap.
Worn ATK / spell ATK / STR Raises offense rating, improving damage roll quality. Most noticeable when fighting higher mitigation targets.
Target avoidance Reduces landed hit rate. Large impact on total DPS when to-hit is modest.
Target mitigation (AC path) Reduces landed hit size. Large impact on max-hit frequency and average hit.

Practical rule in this model: if your hit rate is already strong, more offense rating (ATK/STR) often gives better returns; if your hit rate is weak, improving to-hit inputs can be more valuable first.

12) Displayed ATK in Report

displayedATK = floor((offenseRating + toHit) * 1000 / 744)

This is a reporting transform and not itself the core swing resolution formula.

12.5) Tanking Simulation — NPC Attack Mechanics

The tanking tab models the number of times a mob swings per attack round, matching EQMacEmu's DoMainHandRound.

Base attack count

Mobs can have more than one base attack per round. This is set from the NPC database and entered in the Mob attacks/round field. Each attack rolls through the full hit/avoidance/mitigation chain independently.

NPC double attack

After the base attack loop, the mob makes one double-attack check:

chance = (doubleAttackSkill + level) / 500 [when level > 35, else skill / 500]

If the check succeeds, one additional swing is made. Double-attack skill scales with class and level, capped per class:

NPC classSkill cap
Warrior, Ranger, Rogue245
Paladin, Shadow Knight235
Monk250

When you pick an NPC from the search, the double-attack skill and triple-attack checkbox are filled in automatically based on the NPC's class and level (skill = min(cap, level × 5)).

NPC triple attack

Only Warrior (class 1) and Monk (class 7) NPCs at level 60+ have triple attack. It is only checked on rounds where double attack already succeeded:

P(triple | double succeeds) = 13.5%
P(triple on round) ≈ P(double) × 0.135

13) Tanking Simulation — Avoidance Chain

The tanking sim models each mob swing as a sequential set of independent checks mirroring EQMacEmu's Mob::AvoidDamage. Checks fire in this order; the first one that succeeds negates the swing:

  1. Hit check — does the mob's swing even connect? Uses mob to-hit vs player avoidance (same formula as Section 5). If it misses, the swing is discarded entirely (no avoidance skill roll needed).
  2. Block — rate(blockSkill, divisor=25). Monk only (no shield required).
  3. Parry — rate(parrySkill, divisor=50). Only valid in-front, not ranged.
  4. Riposte — rate(riposteSkill, divisor=55). Requires a weapon equipped; only in-front.
  5. Dodge — rate(dodgeSkill, divisor=45).

The per-skill rate formula (source: attack.cpp Mob::AvoidDamage):

rate(skill, divisor) = min(1, floor((skill + 100) / divisor) / 100)

Each check is independent (not exclusive with others except that the loop stops at the first success). The combined avoidance probability across all four skills for swings that pass the hit check is:

skillAvoidanceRate = 1 − (1−block) × (1−parry) × (1−riposte) × (1−dodge)

AA modifiers to avoidance

  • Combat Agility (Luclin archetype, ranks 1–3): reduces effective hit chance by 2%/5%/10%.
  • Lightning Reflexes (PoP, ranks 1–5): reduces effective hit chance by an additional 2% per rank (stacks with Combat Agility).
hitChance = hitChance − (combatAgilityFactor + lightningReflexesFactor)

14) Tanking Simulation — Mitigation and AC Breakdown

When a swing lands (passes hit check and all avoidance checks), the damage roll uses the same RollD20 contest from Section 5 — mob offense rating vs player mitigation. Player mitigation is built from several components derived from your worn AC, skills, spells, and AGI.

Player AC breakdown (how mitigation is computed)

The tanking report shows a full per-component breakdown:

ComponentFormulaNotes
Item AC (scaled) floor(wornAC × 4/3) Non-casters. Casters use a smaller multiplier. Capped at the anti-twink limit for low-level items.
Defense skill floor(defenseSkill / 3) Divided by 2 for casters. Defense skill scales with level and class.
Spell / buff AC floor(spellAC / 4) Divided by 3 for casters. Buffs like Skin spells and Aegolism contribute here.
AGI bonus floor((AGI − 70) / 20) Only AGI above 70 contributes. Maximum contribution capped by era/class softcap.
Softcap Era- and class-dependent Pre-cap total is clamped to the softcap before AA mitigation modifiers are applied.

The sim report's Player AC section shows each of these intermediate values so you can see exactly how your inputs produce the final mitigation number fed into the RollD20 contest.

AA modifiers to mitigation

  • Combat Stability (Luclin archetype, ranks 1–3): multiplies base player mitigation by 1.02 / 1.05 / 1.10.
  • Innate Defense (PoP, ranks 1–5): adds +2% per rank on top of Combat Stability (stacks).
totalMitigationFactor = combatStabilityFactor + innateDefenseFactor
playerMitigation = floor(playerMitigation × (1 + totalMitigationFactor))

Natural Durability AA (Luclin archetype, ranks 1–3)

Increases player max HP by 2%/5%/10% of the entered HP value before the fight begins.

playerHPTotal = floor(playerHPTotal × (1 + naturalDurabilityFactor))

Planar Durability AA (War/Pal/SHD, PoP, ranks 1–3)

Increases player max HP by an additional 1.5%/3%/4.5% on top of Natural Durability (applied multiplicatively, after Natural Durability).

playerHPTotal = floor(playerHPTotal × (1 + planarDurabilityFactor))

Only applies in the tanking simulation. Only available to Warrior, Paladin, and Shadow Knight.

15) Tanking Simulation — Riposte Counter-Damage

When a riposte succeeds, the mob's swing is negated and the player deals counter-damage using their mainhand weapon and offense stats. The counter-damage uses the same RollD20 / damage roll logic as a player melee swing, but against mob mitigation (derived from mob AC).

Double Riposte AA (Luclin class — War/Pal/Rng/SK/Monk/Rog/Bst)

After a successful riposte counter-attack is rolled, there is an additional chance to perform a second counter-attack on the same swing:

P(double riposte extra hit) = 15% / 35% / 50% at rank 1/2/3

Flash of Steel AA (War/Pal/Rng/SHD/Monk/Rog/Bst, PoP, ranks 1–3): adds +10%/+20%/+30% to the double-riposte chance, stacking additively with Double Riposte.

totalDoubleRiposteChance = doubleRiposteBaseChance + flashOfSteelBonus

The second hit is a full independent riposte damage roll — it does not affect whether the swing was negated (riposte always negates). Both hits are credited to riposteDamageTotal.

Return Kick AA (Luclin class — Monk only)

After a successful riposte, the monk has a chance to automatically perform a bonus Flying Kick as a counter-attack:

P(return kick fires) = 25% / 35% / 50% at rank 1/2/3

Return Kick damage is approximated using the same riposte damage formula (player weapon stats vs mob AC). In the report, Return Kick hits are counted separately under the Riposte section.

16) Tanking Simulation — Survivability

The sim tracks HP across the fight. Each mob swing that lands reduces HP by its rolled damage. Between rounds, HP recovers from regen:

HPgained = netRegenPerSec × (mobAttackDelayMs / 1000)
netRegenPerSec = (playerHPRegen + healerHPS) − 0 (no drain modeled)

When HP hits 0, the sim records the time of death as survival time. If HP never drops to 0, survival time is reported as "survived full fight."

CH chain simulation: The report always computes a Complete Heal chain estimate. Complete Heal fills the tank to full HP (player's total HP). The CH cast time is configurable (default 10s for an unmodified Cleric CH). The estimate shows max safe interval (HP ÷ DTPS), recommended interval (with 1.5× safety factor), clerics needed, and the HP threshold at which a cleric must begin casting.

Key survivability metrics in the report

MetricWhat it means
DTPSDamage taken per second (before regen/heals).
Effective DTPSDTPS minus net regen/healer HPS.
Survival timeSeconds until death with no heals (from HP / DTPS).
Avoidance rate% of swings negated by miss + block + parry + riposte + dodge.
Mitigation %Average reduction on landed hits vs the mob's theoretical max hit.
Riposte DPSOutgoing counter-damage per second from all riposte hits (including Double Riposte and Return Kick).
