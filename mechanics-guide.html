DPS Sim Mechanics Guide

This page explains exactly how this simulator models melee damage, hit chance, haste, offense, and target defenses. Formulas are based on EQMacEmu combat logic and this simulator's implementation.

1) Weapon Base Damage and Delay

Every weapon starts from base damage and delay. Delay is in deciseconds (40 = 4.0s).

effectiveDelayDecisec = max(4, delayDecisec / (1 + effectiveHastePercent / 100))

Faster effective delay means more swings per minute and generally more DPS.

2) Haste

Actual haste rules used by the simulator:

  1. UI worn-haste cap by low level:
    wornHasteUsed = (level <= 25) ? min(wornHasteInput, 10) : wornHasteInput
  2. Raw haste passed into combat:
    rawHaste = wornHasteUsed + spellHasteInput
  3. Positive haste cap by level (combat logic):
    capTotal = 100 + (level > 59 ? 100 : (level > 50 ? 85 : level + 25))
    v3Cap = (level > 50 ? 25 : 10)
    maxBonus = (capTotal - 100) + v3Cap
    effectiveHastePercent = (rawHaste < 0) ? rawHaste : min(rawHaste, maxBonus)
  4. Delay conversion with minimum delay floor:
    effectiveDelayDecisec = max(4, delayDecisec / (1 + effectiveHastePercent / 100))

    Actual result examples:

    delay=40, effectiveHaste=0% -> effectiveDelay=40 decisec (4.00s)
    delay=40, effectiveHaste=41% -> effectiveDelay=28.37 decisec (2.84s)
    delay=10, effectiveHaste=200% -> computed 3.33 decisec, clamped to 4 decisec (0.40s floor)

Haste changes attack frequency, not hit chance directly.

3) Offense Skill vs Offense Rating (Important)

These are different and used in different parts of the calculation:

  • Offense skill (0-255): used in to-hit math.
  • Offense rating: used in damage roll math.
toHit = 7 + offenseSkill + weaponSkill + toHitBonus
offenseRating = offenseSkill + STR_bonus + wornATK + spellATK

STR contributes through:

STR_bonus = floor((2 * STR - 150) / 3), when STR >= 75

4) Hit Chance (Avoidance) vs Damage (Mitigation)

AC is not a single value in this sim. It is split into two defense concepts:

  • Avoidance: controls whether you hit/miss.
  • Mitigation: controls how hard a landed hit is reduced.

Avoidance (hit chance)

effectiveToHit = min(toHit, 550)
a = effectiveToHit + 10; b = avoidance + 10
if a*1.21 > b: hitChance = 1 - b / (a*1.21*2); else hitChance = (a*1.21) / (b*2)

Mitigation (damage size)

Mitigation is the defender value used only for the damage roll (how hard a landed hit hits). It does not affect hit chance. It is computed from mob level and target AC, then used in the RollD20 contest below.

How mitigation is computed (GetMitigation):

  • Level < 15: mit = level × 3 (and +2 if level < 3).
  • Level ≥ 15: mit = floor(level × 41 / 10) − 15.
  • Capped at 200. If the result is 200 and mob AC > 200, use mob AC instead.
  • Then add item/spell AC bonuses: + floor(4×itemAC/3) + floor(spellAC/4).

rollIndex in detail (RollD20)

For each landed hit, the simulator rolls a rollIndex from 1 to 20. That index decides where the hit lands in the damage range:

rollIndex = RollD20(offenseRating, mitigation) // result 1..20
damage = floor((rollIndex × baseDamage + 5) / 10) + damageBonus

So rollIndex = 1 gives minimum damage; rollIndex = 20 gives maximum damage (for that weapon’s base range, before crits etc.).

How rollIndex is derived (the atk/def roll competition):

  1. Attack roll: atkRoll = random(0, offenseRating+5) — uniform integer from 0 up to (offenseRating+5)−1.
  2. Defense roll: defRoll = random(0, mitigation+5) — uniform integer from 0 up to (mitigation+5)−1.
  3. Average scale: avg = floor((offenseRating + mitigation + 10) / 2).
  4. Raw contest: raw = (atkRoll − defRoll) + floor(avg/2), then scaled and clamped into 0..19, and rollIndex = raw + 1 so the final value is 1..20.

What causes a hit to hit for max vs min?

  • High damage (max or near-max): When atkRoll is high and defRoll is low, (atkRoll − defRoll) is large, so the scaled index is high (near 20). So you get max (or near-max) hits when the attacker “wins” the roll contest by a wide margin.
  • Low damage (min or near-min): When atkRoll is low and defRoll is high, (atkRoll − defRoll) is small or negative, so the scaled index is low (near 1). So you get min (or near-min) hits when the defender “wins” the roll contest.

Higher offense rating (STR, worn ATK, spell ATK, offense skill) increases the range of atkRoll, so you can roll higher and get more big hits. Higher mitigation (e.g. high-AC or high-level mobs) increases the range of defRoll, so the defender often “wins” more and you see more low (min) hits and fewer max hits. That is why high-mitigation targets feel like they reduce your damage spread and push hits toward the bottom of the range.

5) In Front vs Behind

Choosing Behind does not force all swings to land. Misses still happen from to-hit vs avoidance.

  • Behind: skips block/parry/riposte/dodge layer.
  • Front: after a successful hit roll, there is an additional avoid chance.
front extra avoid layer = 8%

Use front if you want a conservative/tank-facing estimate; use behind for DPS-from-rear assumptions.

6) Core Max-Hit Formulas

For a single landed non-crit swing before proc damage:

minNonCrit ~= floor((1 * baseDamage + 5) / 10) + damageBonus
maxNonCrit ~= floor((20 * baseDamage + 5) / 10) + damageBonus

So the non-crit range is roughly ~0.1x to ~2.0x of baseDamage (plus damage bonus).

Then multipliers/crit logic can further scale landed damage.

6.5) Double Attack, Triple Attack, and Dual Wield

Double attack (main-hand extra swing)

The simulator computes:

doubleAttackEffective = doubleAttackSkill + level
doubleAttack succeeds if doubleAttackEffective > randomInt(0, 499)

Approximate chance:

P(doubleAttack) ~= doubleAttackEffective / 500

(Capped by the random roll range; bard and beastlord are excluded by sim logic.)

Triple attack (warrior/monk only)

  • Only Warrior and Monk at level 60+.
  • Only checked on rounds where double attack already succeeded.
P(triple | double) = 13.5%
P(triple on round) ~= P(doubleAttack) * 0.135

Dual wield (offhand round chance)

Offhand rounds are gated by dual-wield chance:

dualWieldEffective = dualWieldSkill + level + ambidexterityBonus
dualWield succeeds if dualWieldEffective > randomInt(0, 374)
P(offhand round) ~= dualWieldEffective / 375

Independent offhand timer

Offhand swing timing is independent from main hand.

  • Main hand advances on its own timer from weapon 1 delay.
  • Offhand advances on its own timer from weapon 2 delay.
  • The offhand initial swing is randomly offset, then repeats independently.

This means offhand does not "wait" for main-hand events.

7) Elemental Damage on Weapons

Elemental weapon damage is treated as an extra base-damage adder before the melee damage roll. It is first reduced by target resist for that element (MR/FR/CR/PR/DR).

if resist > 200: elementalDamage = 0
roll = randomInt(1, 201) - resist
if roll < 1: elementalDamage = 0; if 1..99: elementalDamage = floor(weaponElem * roll/100); else elementalDamage = weaponElem

Then this adder is included in swing base damage:

baseDamageForSwing = weaponBaseDamage + elementalDamage(+ bane if enabled)

High target resists can heavily suppress elemental contribution; this is why elemental-heavy weapons can vary by target profile.

8) Proc Chance and Proc Resists

Proc chance per swing/round

Proc chance scales with effective delay and DEX. Offhand has a dual-wield adjustment. Then proc-rate modifiers are applied.

baseProcChance = (0.0004166667 + 1.1437908496732e-5 * DEX) * effectiveDelay
offhandAdjusted = baseProcChance * (50 / dualWieldChance)
finalProcChance = clamp(0, 1, adjustedChance * (100 + procRate) / 100)

Level-gated procs do not fire below required proc level.

Proc spell resist resolution

For resistable proc spells, the sim checks immunity and then runs a resist calculation using target resist type (MR/FR/CR/DR/PR), level delta, and spell resist modifier.

resistChance = clamp(0, 200, targetResist + levelMod + resistModifier)
if roll > resistChance: full land (100% damage)
if roll <= resistChance: full resist (0%) or partial based on no-partial flag

Resulting proc damage is:

actualProcDamage = floor(baseProcDamage * effectiveness / 100)

So procs can be:

  • Full land: full listed proc damage.
  • Partial resist: reduced proc damage.
  • Full resist: 0 proc damage.

This is why two weapons with similar listed proc damage can perform differently against different target resist profiles.

9) What ATK and AC Usually Do (Prioritization)

Stat Main Effect When It Matters Most
Offense skill / weapon skill Raises hit chance via to-hit. Most noticeable when accuracy is not near cap.
Worn ATK / spell ATK / STR Raises offense rating, improving damage roll quality. Most noticeable when fighting higher mitigation targets.
Target avoidance Reduces landed hit rate. Large impact on total DPS when to-hit is modest.
Target mitigation (AC path) Reduces landed hit size. Large impact on max-hit frequency and average hit.

Practical rule in this model: if your hit rate is already strong, more offense rating (ATK/STR) often gives better returns; if your hit rate is weak, improving to-hit inputs can be more valuable first.

10) Displayed ATK in Report

displayedATK = floor((offenseRating + toHit) * 1000 / 744)

This is a reporting transform and not itself the core swing resolution formula.
